# reference/wattlebrook-past-promotions.md: what has been tried before

The four past results Ross measures every new proposal against. He remembers the first two the way people remember car accidents. All figures exclude GST.

## 1. The May voucher (two years ago)

- **What it was:** "$10 off any order", no minimum, mailed to the whole active file of 101,000.
- **What the board was told:** a record month. May sales $3.42 million against a typical $2.61 million, up 31%.
- **What happened next:** June $2.14 million (down 18%). July $2.30 million (down 12%).
- **The quarter:** $7.86 million against a normal $7.83 million. Up $30,000.
- **The give:** 29,400 vouchers redeemed, $294,000.
- **The result:** about $18,000 of extra margin bought for $294,000. Roughly $276,000 worse off.
- **Where the vouchers went:** 62% were redeemed by customers who had ordered in the previous 90 days. They were coming anyway.
- **What Ross took from it:** a voucher moves orders forward in time and pays regular customers to do what they were already doing. And a record month can be a loss.

## 2. The paid social push (last year)

- **What it was:** an agency ran four months of social media advertising to find new customers.
- **Cost:** $180,000 of advertising and $36,000 of fees. $216,000.
- **What the agency reported:** a return of 4.2, which would be $756,000 of sales.
- **What Priya matched back:** 2,050 new customers. Average first order $58. First-order sales $118,900.
- **Cost per new customer:** $105.
- **Margin on the first order after pick, pack and freight:** $25.58.
- **Repeat:** 71% never ordered a second time within nine months. About 590 did.
- **What Ross took from it:** a platform's own report is an advertisement for the platform. Count the orders yourself.

## 3. Last March's lapsed mailing (no voucher)

- **What it was:** the March catalogue sent to 18,000 lapsed customers, last order 13 to 24 months before. No offer.
- **Response:** 3.2%. 576 orders. Average order $88.
- **Result:** it roughly paid for its own postage.
- **Afterwards:** 44% of those 576 ordered again within six months, at full price. Those second orders came off later catalogues, each with its own cost.
- **Why it matters:** this is what lapsed customers do with no voucher at all. It is the number any voucher has to beat.

## 4. The October voucher test (five months ago)

- **What it was:** the current Marketing Manager mailed 2,000 lapsed customers the October catalogue with a $10 voucher.
- **Result as reported:** 112 orders, 5.6%.
- **What Ross thinks of it:** he heard about it in a corridor and has never seen it written up. 112 orders is a small number. There was no group mailed without the voucher, so nobody knows what the catalogue alone would have done. October carries the joint-care feature and is the strongest catalogue of the year. He does not know whether the voucher had a minimum order, or what those 112 customers did afterwards.
- **What it would take to change his mind:** the same test with a plain group beside it.
