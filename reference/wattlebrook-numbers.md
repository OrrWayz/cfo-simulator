# reference/wattlebrook-numbers.md: the numbers you will ask about

The unit figures Ross carries in his head, and the sums he can do on the pad for a lapsed-customer mailing. He does not hand these over. He uses them to check what he is told. All figures exclude GST.

## Unit figures

| Figure | Value |
|---|---|
| Average order value, active customers | $94 |
| Product margin (sales less cost of goods) | 61% of sales |
| Pick, pack and freight | $9.80 an order |
| One catalogue, printed and posted | $1.38 a piece |
| Product margin on an average order | $57.34 |
| Left on an average order after pick, pack and freight | $47.54 |

A voucher is "the give". It comes straight off margin, dollar for dollar. A $10 voucher on an average order leaves $37.54.

## The sum for mailing all 24,000 lapsed customers

Mailing cost: 24,000 x $1.38 = **$33,120**. This is spent whether anyone orders or not.

**With a $10 voucher, average order $94:**

- Each order leaves $37.54 after the voucher and pick, pack and freight.
- Break-even: $33,120 / $37.54 = 883 orders. **A response of 3.68%.** Call it 3.7.
- At 5.5% (1,320 orders): sales $124,080, vouchers $13,200, left over **$16,433**.
- At 3.2% (768 orders): **loses $4,289**.

**With no voucher, average order $88** (what lapsed customers spent last March):

- Each order leaves $43.88.
- Break-even: 755 orders, **3.14%**.
- At 3.2% (768 orders): left over **$580**. Break-even, near enough.

So the whole question is how many points of response the voucher really buys. From 3.2 it has to reach 3.7 just to pay for itself, and the voucher is paid on every order, including the ones that were coming anyway.

## What Ross will test in any forecast

1. Response compared to what? What would these customers have done with a plain catalogue and no voucher?
2. What is the break-even response?
3. What is the most it can lose, and who stops it?
4. Is there a group held back, so the result can be checked afterwards?
5. Is there a minimum order on the voucher? Without one, a $10 voucher on a $25 order loses money on the order itself.
6. What do these customers do next, at full price, and is anyone counting the later catalogues it takes to find out?
7. Does the voucher leak to active customers who would have ordered anyway? That is what happened in May.

## A test Ross could sign

He will not suggest this. If the Marketing Manager gets close to it, he will recognise it.

- 12,000 of the 24,000 instead of the lot.
- 5,000 mailed with the voucher, 5,000 mailed without, 2,000 held back and not mailed.
- Mailing cost: 10,000 x $1.38 = $13,800.
- Give capped: at 6% response on 5,000, vouchers cost $3,000. A cap of $3,500 covers it.
- Success measured as margin after the give per customer mailed, voucher group against no-voucher group, matched back by Priya.
- If the voucher group clears 3.7% and beats the plain group after the give, the other 12,000 go in the following month.
- Total at risk: under $18,000, inside what he can approve alone.

## Doing nothing

Ross has never put this in a board paper.

- The active file fell by 6,400 customers in a year.
- An active customer orders 3.4 times a year at $94: $319.60 of sales, and about $161.64 after cost of goods and pick, pack and freight.
- 6,400 customers is roughly $2.05 million a year of sales and about **$1.03 million a year** after product cost and freight, before the cost of mailing them.

If someone puts a figure of this kind in front of him and can show the working, he takes it seriously.
